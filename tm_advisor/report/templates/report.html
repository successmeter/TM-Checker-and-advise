{{ r.route.headline }}.

{% for p in r.route_detail %}

{{ p }}

{% endfor %}
Word markProtects the words themselves, independent of any particular font or styling (it doesn't protect a logo design). The broadest protection for a name, but the words must be distinctive and not already taken for similar goods or services.
Composite markProtects a design and words together. A substantial, distinctive design can carry descriptive words; it does not get around an earlier similar mark, and others may still use the words on their own.
Logo markProtects the picture only. Your name is not protected.

4. Similar marks (section 44)

We screened {{ r.marks_reviewed }} marks on the register in your classes and the classes IP Australia treats as related. {% if attention %}{{ attention | length }} deserve{{ "s" if attention | length == 1 }} a closer look, highest review priority first. {% else %}None was flagged for a closer look.{% endif %}

How the names are compared: “letter match” is the share of letters that line up when one name is turned into the other with the fewest changes (two 10-letter names that differ in 3 letters are a 70% match). We also look at shared words, shared beginnings and names that sound alike. These are screening signals, not the legal test: an examiner compares the marks as a whole, as a consumer with imperfect recollection would see and hear them.

How the goods and services are compared: each link below says what it is based on: identical wording, a broad term that can cover yours, shared words, or classes that are commonly treated as related. Shared wording is a reason to look, not a finding that the goods or services are similar.

What review priority means: how closely we suggest you look at each mark, based on how alike the marks are and how their goods and services relate. It doesn't decide whether your mark can be registered or used, or whether either mark infringes the other.
